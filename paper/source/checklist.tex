\section*{NeurIPS Paper Checklist}

\begin{enumerate}

\item {\bf Claims}
    \item[] Question: Do the main claims made in the abstract and introduction accurately reflect the paper's contributions and scope?
    \item[] Answer: \answerYes{} % Replace by \answerYes{}, \answerNo{}, or \answerNA{}.
    \item[] Justification: The abstract and Section~\ref{sec:intro} accurately describe the paper's two contributions: (i) the asymptotic comparison of pooled and ensemble ridge regression under covariate shift, formalized in Theorems~\ref{thm:gen_ensemble},~\ref{thm:ensemble_optimal},~\ref{thm:Pooled}, and Corollary~\ref{cor:comp_k2_iso_re}, and (ii) the simulation study spanning underparameterized, interpolation, and overparameterized regimes presented in Section~\ref{sec:sim-setup} and Figure~\ref{fig:F1}.
   

\item {\bf Limitations}
    \item[] Question: Does the paper discuss the limitations of the work performed by the authors?
    \item[] Answer: \answerYes{} % Replace by \answerYes{}, \answerNo{}, or \answerNA{}.
    \item[] Justification: A dedicated ``Limitations and future work'' paragraph at the end of Section~4 explicitly discusses the well-specified linear model assumption, the shared coefficients and noise variance across clusters, the restriction to mean-shift covariate heterogeneity, the assumption of known cluster memberships, and the random-effects setting under which the optimally weighted optimally tuned comparison is stated. The remark following Assumption~\ref{assumptions} additionally clarifies the scope of the modeling choices.
    
\item {\bf Theory assumptions and proofs}
    \item[] Question: For each theoretical result, does the paper provide the full set of assumptions and a complete (and correct) proof?
    \item[] Answer: \answerYes{} % Replace by \answerYes{}, \answerNo{}, or \answerNA{}.
    \item[] Justification: All assumptions are collected in Assumption~\ref{assumptions} (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), and each theorem statement explicitly references it. Full proofs of Theorems~\ref{thm:gen_ensemble}, ~\ref{thm:ensemble_optimal}, ~\ref{thm:Pooled}, and Corollary~\ref{cor:comp_k2_iso_re} are provided in Appendix~A.3 through A.6, supported by Lemmas~\ref{lemma:hn} and~\ref{lemma:hn_gn} and the background result Proposition~\ref{prop:prop_anisotropic_local_law} from \citet{knowles2017anisotropic}.
    
    \item {\bf Experimental result reproducibility}
    \item[] Question: Does the paper fully disclose all the information needed to reproduce the main experimental results of the paper to the extent that it affects the main claims and/or conclusions of the paper (regardless of whether the code and data are provided or not)?

    \item[] Justification: Section~\ref{sec:sim-setup} specifies the full simulation protocol (cluster sizes, covariate distributions, coefficient generation, signal-to-noise ratio, test set construction, range of $p$, and number of repetitions), and Section~\ref{sec:sim-hetero} specifies the parameter configurations. The ``Base Learners'' paragraph in Section~\ref{sec:sim-setup} specifies the numerical routines used for both ridge and ridgeless learners, including the cross-validation grid for the ridge tuning parameter. 
    
\item {\bf Open access to data and code}
    \item[] Question: Does the paper provide open access to the data and code, with sufficient instructions to faithfully reproduce the main experimental results, as described in supplemental material?
    \item[] Answer: \answerYes{} % Replace by \answerYes{}, \answerNo{}, or \answerNA{}.
    \item[] Justification: We have included all code for replicating analyses and figures as supplementary material.
    

\item {\bf Experimental setting/details}
    \item[] Question: Does the paper specify all the training and test details (e.g., data splits, hyperparameters, how they were chosen, type of optimizer) necessary to understand the results?
    \item[] Answer: \answerYes{} % Replace by \answerYes{}, \answerNo{}, or \answerNA{}.
    \item[] Justification: Section~\ref{sec:sim-setup} specifies the data-generating process, sample sizes, covariate dimension grid, signal-to-noise ratio, and number of repetitions. The ``Base Learners'' paragraph in Section~\ref{sec:sim-setup} specifies the use  of \texttt{numpy.linalg.lstsq} for the ridgeless solution and \texttt{RidgeCV} from \texttt{scikit-learn}~\citep{pedregosa2011scikit} with a logarithmic grid of 20 values over $[10^{-6}, 10^{6}]$ tuned via leave-one-out cross-validation independently for every learner.
   

\item {\bf Experiment statistical significance}
    \item[] Question: Does the paper report error bars suitably and correctly defined or other appropriate information about the statistical significance of the experiments?
    \item[] Answer: \answerYes{} % Replace by \answerYes{}, \answerNo{}, or \answerNA{}.
    \item[] Justification:  As described in Section~\ref{sec:sim-setup}, all reported quantities are averaged over 100 independent repetitions, and 95\% confidence intervals computed from the $t$-distribution are plotted in Figure~\ref{fig:F1}. Section~4 explicitly references these confidence intervals when discussing the lack of effect of cluster geometry.
    

\item {\bf Experiments compute resources}
    \item[] Question: For each experiment, does the paper provide sufficient information on the computer resources (type of compute workers, memory, time of execution) needed to reproduce the experiments?
    \item[] Answer: \answerYes{} % Replace by \answerYes{}, \answerNo{}, or \answerNA{}.
    \item[] Justification: As stated at the beginning of Section~3, all experiments were run using 4 CPU cores on a local machine. The simulations involve ridge and ridgeless least-squares fits on synthetic data with $n = 2000$ and $p$ up to $6000$, and complete within standard local-machine time and memory.
