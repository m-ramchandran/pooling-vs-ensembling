SUPPLEMENTAL SIMULATIONS -- Ensemble vs Pooled, 95% t confidence intervals

Design: $K \\in \\{2,3,4,5\\}$ clusters, $n_\\ell = 400$ training (pooled sample size is $400 \\times K$) and 200 test points each, every scenario run over 100 replicates. Means are drawn from $\\mu_\\ell \\sim N(0, 9 I_p)$, so
clusters are well separated.

A point from cluster $\\ell$ is $x = \\mu_\\ell + z_\\ell$, where $z_\\ell$ has mean zero and
covariance $\\Sigma_\\ell$. The seven different simulation scenarios below
differ only in the distribution of $z_\\ell$, and the first four give every cluster the same covariance:

- identity: $z_\\ell \\sim N(0, I)$.
- equicorrelated: $z_\\ell \\sim N(0, \\Sigma)$ with
  $\\Sigma = (1-\\rho)I + \\rho\\mathbf{1}\\mathbf{1}^{\\top}$, at $\\rho = 0.2, 0.5$ and $0.8$.
- spiked: $z_\\ell \\sim N(0, WW^{\\top} + I)$, with $W$ a $p \\times \\mathrm{round}(p/10)$
  standard Gaussian drawn once per replicate. For this structure and the cluster-specific
  version below, $\\beta$ is drawn in $\\mathrm{span}(W)$, following Hastie et al. (2022).
- non-gaussian: $z_\\ell = \\tanh(Wu)/0.628$ with $u \\sim N(0, I_p)$ and $W$ a $p \\times p$
  matrix of $N(0, 1/p)$ entries, the constant restoring unit variance per coordinate. There
  is no $\\Sigma_\\ell$ here, so this case leaves Assumption 2.1 altogether. 
  The outcome is still exactly linear in the observed $x$.

Three let the covariance differ across clusters, which is the case Remark 2.2 leaves open:

- diagonal, per-cluster scale: $\\Sigma_\\ell = c_\\ell I$, with $c_\\ell$ the first $K$ of
  $(0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8)$, so the spread across clusters is $2\\times$ at $K = 2$ and
  $16\\times$ at $K = 5$.
- equicorrelated, cluster-specific $\\rho_\\ell$:
  $\\Sigma_\\ell = (1-\\rho_\\ell)I + \\rho_\\ell\\mathbf{1}\\mathbf{1}^{\\top}$, with
  $\\rho_\\ell$ the first $K$ of $(0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9)$.
- low-rank pertubation, cluster-specific $W_\\ell$: $\\Sigma_\\ell = W_\\ell W_\\ell^{\\top} + I$ with
  $W_\\ell = W + 0.5 E_\\ell$ and $E_\\ell$ standard Gaussian, so the perturbed directions
  themselves move between clusters.

Outcome: $y = x^{\\top}\\beta_\\ell + \\varepsilon$ with
$\\varepsilon \\sim N(0, \\sigma_\\ell^2)$, and $\\beta_\\ell$ either shared or
$(-1)^\\ell\\beta$. Here $\\beta$ is scaled so that $\\beta^{\\top}\\Sigma_\\ell\\beta$ averages
1 over clusters in every structure. Noise is either $\\sigma_\\ell^2 = 1$ throughout, or varies across clusters spanning  $16\\times$ with $\\mathrm{mean}(\\sigma_\\ell^2) = 1$, holding both the average
level and the max/min ratio fixed as $K$ varies.

The $p$ grid runs 20 to 800 in steps of 40, with 392 and 408 added to straddle $p = 400$, plus
ten multipliers of $400 \\times K$, from $0.6$ to $1.4$.

The tables below reports the ensemble test MSE divided by pooled test MSE (> 1 means pooling is superior), for ridgeless learners with equal noise and shared $\\beta$. We report the mean and 95%
t-intervals over the 100 replicates. 

Numbering: the number is the scenario, the letter is the estimator
(a = ridgeless, b = ridge).
  T1  covariance structure x k, equal noise variance
  T2  covariance structure x k, 16x spread in sigma^2
  T3  the two interpolation peaks (identity)
  T4  ensembling scheme under noise heterogeneity (identity)
  T5  shared vs opposite-sign beta (identity)
T1, T2, T3 and T5 report whichever of the three ensembling schemes does
best (simple average, inverse-variance, ridge stacking). T4 instead breaks
simple-average and inverse-variance out separately, which is what shows
whether inverse-variance weighting rescues the ensemble under unequal
noise; it is not a subset of T2.
